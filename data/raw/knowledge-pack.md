# Passport fees — how much a passport costs and how to pay
The standard first passport (34-page A Series ordinary) costs KES 7,550 on eCitizen (gazetted KES 7,500 plus the KES 50 eCitizen convenience fee; Kenya Gazette Notice 15493 of November 2023, in force since 1 March 2024). Larger booklets: 50-page B Series KES 9,550; 66-page C Series KES 12,550. Replacement of a lost or mutilated passport: KES 20,050. Booklet availability on eCitizen has fluctuated throughout the 2025–26 booklet shortage — at times only one booklet size has been selectable — so the fee shown at the eCitizen payment step is the authoritative price. Payment is via M-Pesa, Airtel Money, debit/credit card, or online banking through the eCitizen gateway. Two payment invoices are issued after paying — download and print both before closing the tab.

# Processing timelines — how long it takes to get the passport
Official timelines differ slightly between government sources: the eCitizen portal quotes 10 working days for first-time applications, the DIS Service Delivery Charter says 14 working days, and the DIS FAQ says fourteen (14) days from biometric capture — plan for roughly two to three weeks. A gazetted Express Passport tier exists — 34-page booklet at KES 30,000 (Kenya Gazette Notice 15493 of November 2023). In practice, urgent cases with documentary proof (medical emergency, studies, or a job abroad) are handled at a dedicated desk at Nyayo House, Nairobi; in March 2026 the government announced a 72-hour channel for applicants with jobs abroad. Diaspora applications through High Commissions and Embassies take longer, roughly 4–8 weeks.

# Processing centres — where to book your appointment
There are ten passport processing centres where you can book your biometrics appointment: Nairobi (Nyayo House), Kisumu, Mombasa, Eldoret, Nakuru, Embu, Kisii, Kericho, Bungoma, and Garissa (opened February 2025). You may book any centre through eCitizen — pick the most convenient. The passport is collected at the same centre where your biometrics were taken. Applicants near Thika typically book Nairobi (Nyayo House), about 40 km away; if Nyayo House appointment slots are congested, Embu (about 90 km) is the nearest alternative. Diaspora applicants use the nearest Kenya High Commission or Embassy.

# Documents to bring to the appointment — first-time adult applicant
Bring to your biometrics appointment: original birth certificate and a copy; original national ID and a copy; the printed eCitizen application form (Form 19) and both payment invoices. You do not need printed passport photos — your photo is captured live at the biometrics appointment (the online form may ask for a digital photo upload, and diaspora applicants at embassies may still need printed photos). Warning: a name or date-of-birth mismatch between your national ID and birth certificate causes rejection — correct the birth certificate at Civil Registration Services or the ID at the National Registration Bureau before applying. Minors need extra documents including parental consent.

# Application flow — the steps from start to finish
Register or log in on eCitizen at dis.ecitizen.go.ke, open Directorate of Immigration Services, choose New Applications, complete Form 19 online and upload the required documents, pay the fee, download and print the two payment invoices, book an appointment at one of the ten processing centres, attend in person for biometric capture (photo and fingerprints), and collect the passport from that same centre once notified (carry your invoice and original national ID). The Recommending Officer section of Form 19 is optional since July 2026 — an application is no longer held up for lack of a recommender.
